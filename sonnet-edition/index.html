The Anti-Translation Sprint

“Pour a fresh cup of Earl Grey and let English become a melody, not a puzzle.”

Our mission: fluidity over translation. Never pause to translate into or out of your first language. Follow the 3-second rule:

  1. Open your mouth within 3 seconds.
  2. Keep talking for 20–30 seconds.
  3. Don't hunt for single words. Describe the mechanism, purpose or feeling in simple, confident English.

The 3-Angle Definition Formula

When Lewis throws a surprise prompt at you, launch straight into one of these:

1 · Functional

“It essentially refers to a state or situation where…”

Complacency essentially refers to a state where people feel too comfortable to notice warning signs.

2 · Contrast

“It's practically the polar opposite of X. Rather than Y, it involves…”

A summit is the polar opposite of a sleepover. Rather than lounging about, it involves serious decisions.

3 · Vignette

“Picture a scenario where… that's the textbook definition of it.”

Picture a brand releasing a controversial advert and getting thousands of complaints overnight. That's a textbook backlash.

Pronunciation & Flow

1 · Stress-timed rhythm

Some languages are syllable-timed: every syllable gets equal weight, da-da-da-da. British English is stress-timed: only key content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives) get a strong beat. Everything else is squashed and quick.

Glide over the little words so the strong beats land in a steady tempo.

2 · The schwa /ə/

The most common sound in English. Lips neutral, jaw loose, tongue relaxed in the middle.

community /kəˈmjuːnəti/suppose /səˈpəʊz/observe /əbˈzɜːv/woman /ˈwʊmən/construction /kənˈstrʌkʃən/convince /kənˈvɪns/

Endings: -shire /ʃə/ (Yorkshire, Hampshire, Oxfordshire) · -mouth /məθ/ (Bournemouth, Portsmouth, Dartmouth)

3 · Catenation, joining words like ribbon ‿

If the next word starts with a vowel sound, the last consonant slides across to meet it.
