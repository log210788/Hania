## 🎼 Part 1: Pitch Contours & Implicature

In advanced British English (SSBE / Modern RP), **how** your pitch moves on the **tonic syllable** carries more social meaning than the vocabulary itself.

| Contour | Symbol | Pragmatic Meaning | Model Sentence |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **High Fall** | `↘` | Finality, confidence, crisp assertion | *"That's **↘settled** then."* |
| **Fall-Rise** | `↘↗` | Polite reservation, unspoken *"but..."*, partial agreement | *"Well, it's **↘↗interesting**..."* *(implying: "...though flawed.")* |
| **Low Rise** | `↗` | Non-threatening continuation, open invitation | *"If you have a **↗moment**..."* |
| **Rise-Fall** | `↗↘` | Impressed surprise, or cuttingly dry sarcasm | *"How **↗↘fascinating**."* |

### Contrastive Tonic Focus Drill
Say the exact same sentence 4 ways, shifting the tonic stress (`**CAPS**`) and using a **Fall-Rise (`↘↗`)**:
1. *"I didn't say **HE** orchestrated it."* → (Someone else did.)
2. *"I didn't **SAY** he orchestrated it."* → (I only implied it.)
3. *"I didn't say he **ORCHESTRATED** it."* → (He merely went along with it.)
4. *"I **DIDN'T** say he orchestrated it."* → (Strong denial / correcting the record.)

## 🫖 Part 2: C1–C2 Diplomatic Subtext & Understatement

Match the surface British phrase to its **actual pragmatic weight** in a high-stakes meeting or social salon:

1. *"With the greatest respect, I'm not entirely convinced that's the whole picture."*
   - **Subtext**: Your argument has a major blind spot.
2. *"That's certainly one way of looking at it."*
   - **Subtext**: I strongly disagree, but I'm keeping the room civil.
3. *"I wouldn't go so far as to call it a disaster, though it leaves a fair bit to be desired."*
   - **Subtext**: It's a serious mess that needs immediate repair.
4. *"Let's perhaps park that for now and revisit it once the dust settles."*
   - **Subtext**: This idea is dead on arrival; let's move on gracefully.
5. *"It's not altogether out of the question, provided we iron out a few creases."*
   - **Subtext**: Yes, *only* if you fix these specific problems first.

## 🔗 Part 3: Advanced Connected Speech (Intrusive `/r/` & Yod-Coalescence)

Recycle Week 1 **catenation** and Week 2 **elision/gemination**, and layer on two hallmarks of effortless SSBE:

### A. Linking & Intrusive `/r/`
When a word ends in `/ə/`, `/ɔː/`, or `/ɑː/` and the next begins with a vowel, SSBE inserts a soft `/r/` glide (never rolled):
- *law **‿r‿** and order* (`/ˈlɔː r‿ən ˈɔːdə/`)
- *the idea **‿r‿** of it* (`/ði aɪˈdɪə r‿əv ɪt/`)
- *media **‿r‿** attention* (`/ˈmiːdiə r‿əˈtenʃən/`)
- *I saw **‿r‿** a film* (`/aɪ ˈsɔː r‿ə fɪlm/`)
- *vanilla **‿r‿** ice cream* (`/vəˈnɪlə r‿aɪs kriːm/`)

### B. Yod-Coalescence (`/t/ + /j/ → /tʃ/`, `/d/ + /j/ → /dʒ/`)
- *would you mind* → `/wʊdʒu ˈmaɪnd/`
- *did you manage* → `/dɪdʒu ˈmænɪdʒ/`
- *last year's summit* → `/ˌlɑːstʃɪəz ˈsʌmɪt/`
- *I bet you* → `/aɪ ˈbetʃu/`
